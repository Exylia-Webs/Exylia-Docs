The area is the box of blocks that is the mine. You mark two corners with ExyliaLib's selection wand, no WorldEdit needed, and optionally cut the box into a sphere or a cylinder.

## Before you start

Create the mine first, see [Create and edit mines](/docs/exyliamines/mines). You do not need an area to create it, but a mine without one cannot fill, reset or be mined.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the area section">
    Run `/minesadmin`, left-click your mine, then click **Mine ▸ Area**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark the region">
    Click **Area ▸ Mine region**. You get the selection wand: mark two opposite corners of the box and confirm. Both corners are part of the mine and it does not matter which one you mark first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a shape (optional)">
    Click **Area ▸ Shape** to cycle between the shapes below. A new mine is a cuboid.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a hologram (optional)">
    Stand where you want it and left-click **Area ▸ Hologram**. See [Holograms](/docs/exyliamines/holograms).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Selecting again later corrects the corners and keeps the shape the mine already had. Backing out of the selection leaves the old region as it was.

## Check that it works

Back in `/minesadmin` the mine shows its region as set. For a classic mine, click **Resets ▸ Force reset** and the whole shape fills (see [Set up a classic mine](/docs/exyliamines/classic-mines)). Then break a block inside it as a player: it breaks, pays its drops and counts.

## Shapes

**Area ▸ Shape** cycles through three, each cut out of the box you selected:

| Shape | What the mine is |
|---|---|
| **Cuboid** | The whole box. The default. |
| **Sphere** | The largest sphere that fits in the box, centred in it: as wide as the box's shortest side. |
| **Cylinder** | An upright cylinder as wide as the box's narrower horizontal side and as tall as the box. |

A block belongs to a rounded mine when its centre is inside the shape. The corners of the box outside it are not part of the mine: a refill leaves them alone, breaking there is not the mine's business, and a player standing there is not inside it.

<Callout type="tip" title="A sphere wants a cube">
The sphere is as wide as the box's **shortest** side. Select a box as tall as it is wide and deep, or the sphere comes out smaller than you expect.
</Callout>

## A world that loads late

A mine whose world is not loaded simply waits. With a world manager such as Worlds or Multiverse-Core that loads worlds after the plugins, the mine's area is registered the moment its world loads, and the realistic blocks it was regrowing there are picked up again.

## How a break inside a mine works

Inside an enabled mine, the server's own break is **always cancelled** for every player, and the mine performs the break itself. This is what lets a mine coexist with everything else on the server:

- **Anticheats and other guards** decide first. The plugin listens at `LOW` priority and ignores breaks already cancelled, so a fast-break or nuker check that refuses a break is respected.
- **Protection plugins** such as WorldGuard decide at `NORMAL` and see the break already cancelled. There is no flag to set for mines and no fight over who wins: a mine never forces a cancelled break through.
- **The mine applies its own rules**: its permission, its blocks, its loot, its regeneration.

When the mine performs the break it reproduces what the server would have done: drops worked out with the tool in hand (Fortune, Silk Touch and the correct-tool rules included), durability (Unbreaking applies, the tool breaks with its sound), hunger exhaustion, and experience. Experience goes **straight to the miner**, Mending included, instead of dropping an orb. A player in creative mode gets no drops and spends no durability, as in vanilla.

<Callout type="warning" title="Block loggers and listeners that skip cancelled events">
Because the server's break is cancelled, a plugin that ignores cancelled break events never sees a mine break, a block logger for instance. Plugins that want to react to mine breaks listen to `MineBlockBreakEvent` instead, see [API](/docs/exyliamines/api). Block-break statistics and advancements are not credited either.
</Callout>

### Tools that break several blocks

A 3x3 pickaxe or a vein miner from another plugin should hand each block to the mine through the [API](/docs/exyliamines/api). Each one is then treated as a swing of its own, with the permission check, the loot and the regeneration, so a 3x3 pickaxe in a mine is nine breaks rather than one break and eight holes the mine never refills.

## What cannot farm a mine

A mine pays for and regrows only blocks it put there. Every way to slip one in is closed:

| What | What happens |
|---|---|
| Placing one of the mine's own blocks inside it | Refused with "The mine's own blocks can't be placed inside it." Anything else may be placed. Admins are exempt. |
| Lava and water meeting inside it | A stone, cobblestone or obsidian that would form is cancelled when it is one of the mine's blocks. |
| Explosions | Blocks inside a mine are taken out of the explosion; everything around it is still hit. |
| Pistons | A piston may neither pull a block out of a mine nor push anything into it. |

Without these, an ore carried in with Silk Touch would pay the mine's loot again, count towards its reset and, in a realistic mine, make that spot regrow the ore for good.

## Overlapping mines

Two mines may overlap, but avoid it: a block in the overlap belongs to whichever enabled mine the region index answers with first, and a player is only ever in one mine's session at a time. Walking out of one of two overlapping mines keeps the session of the one the player is still in.
