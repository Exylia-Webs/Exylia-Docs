In a few minutes you can tell whether the server is growing, whether new players stay, where they come from, and
whether a campaign paid for itself. These pages read the **network sessions** (from the proxy or a standalone
server) and the **presence** on each game server. Hostname, country, language, client version and Java/Bedrock come
from the session a player opened; playtime, AFK and activity from the presence.

## Before you start

You need a linked proxy or standalone server that has seen players join, see
[Connect your server](/docs/exyliaanalytics/linking). A new link needs days of data before comebacks and
retention fill in; a chart saying *"Collecting since ..."* is normal at the start. To judge campaigns, point each
place you advertise at its own hostname on your proxy.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check growth">
    Open **Players › Growth**. **Is the server growing?** reads the last 8 full weeks as Growing, Stable or
    Declining. **Players gained per player lost** above 1 means the base grows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that new players stay">
    Open **Retention**. Read **Played again on Day 1**, **Day 7** and **Day 30**, and the dashed target lines (40%,
    20% and 10%).
  </Step>
  <Step title="See where players come from">
    Open **Player sources**. **Sources and campaigns** lists where new players first joined from, with how many
    played 10+ minutes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a campaign">
    On **Player sources › Campaigns**, pick **New campaign** and choose a channel (Discord, YouTube, Ads, Server
    list or Something else). Fill in **Name**, **Start of the address** and **Your main server address**, then
    **Create campaign**. Create the address it shows under **Link to hand out** in your DNS and point it at your
    proxy, otherwise players cannot join with it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record what you paid">
    Use **Record a cost**: choose the **Campaign**, the **Day**, the **Amount** and a **Note**, then **Add cost**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check that it works

**Growth** shows a line for new players and a verdict on the momentum. **Retention** shows the three Day numbers
once enough days have passed (a cell that is not there yet shows `—`, never `0%`). After you point a hostname at a
campaign and a player joins through it, the campaign's **New players** counts one.

## Going further

### Definitions

The same words mean the same thing on every page.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Active player | Has a network session that started that day. |
| DAU / WAU / MAU | Unique active players in the trailing 1 / 7 / 30 days. |
| Stickiness | Average DAU ÷ MAU over the range. |
| Playtime | Session time minus AFK time. AFK time is shown on its own. |
| Players online | From the regular check-ins: the proxy's count on a network, the sum of standalone servers otherwise. |
| Identity | Bedrock when any of the player's sessions was reported by Floodgate, or their UUID is a Floodgate one (`00000000-0000-0000-…`). Cracked when the UUID is an offline-mode one made from the name. Premium otherwise. A Bedrock player linked to a Java account keeps the Java UUID and counts as Bedrock only where the agent could ask Floodgate. |

### Growth accounting

| Group | Definition |
|---|---|
| New | First session ever in the bucket. |
| Kept playing (retained) | Active in the bucket and in the one before. |
| Returned after a break (resurrected) | Active, not new, and inactive for at least 30 days before. |
| Came back (returning) | Active, and none of the above. |
| Gone (churned) | Last session exactly 30 days before the bucket ends: a player churns the day they reach 30 days away. Drawn below the line. |
| Players gained per player lost (quick ratio) | (New + Returned after a break) ÷ Gone. Above 1 the base grows. |

### Lifecycle segments

Decided as of the end of the range, in this order, each player in one segment only:

| Segment | Definition |
|---|---|
| New | First seen less than 7 days ago. |
| Gone (churned) | Last seen more than 30 days ago. |
| Fading away (at risk) | Last seen 7 to 30 days ago. |
| Loyal (core) | Active on 12 or more of the last 30 days. |
| Regular | Active on 4 to 11 of them. |
| Occasional (casual) | Active on 1 to 3 of them. |

### Growth

Open **Players › Growth**.

| Chart | Shows |
|---|---|
| Is the server growing? | The weekly trend over the last 8 full weeks: Growing above +2% per week, Stable, or Declining below -2%. |
| Players gained per player lost | Players gained for every player lost over the range, with the break-even line at 1. |
| New, regular and returning players | Who the active players of each period are. |
| The player base over time | Players in each lifecycle segment at the end of every period; gone players are left out. |
| New players by identity | Premium, Cracked and Bedrock among new players. |
| Where players are heading | Daily active players projected ahead as a straight line through the range, with a 95% band. No seasonality: a weekend pattern shows up as noise. Needs enough days in the range. |

### Hours and playtime

Open **Activity › Hours & playtime**.

| Chart | Shows |
|---|---|
| Time played | Per player per day (AFK left out), visits per player per day, and total playtime. |
| When players are online | Average players online per weekday and hour: the best time for events and restarts. |
| How long visits last | Each visit bucketed into under 5 min, 5–15, 15–30, 30–60 min, 1–2 h, 2–4 h and over 4 h, with the median. |
| Where players spend their time | Hours on each game server per period, AFK included. Needs game servers that report joins and leaves. |
| What players do per hour played | Chat messages, commands, deaths and player kills per hour of playtime, from the counters each server reports when a player leaves it. |
| Who does most of the playing | The share of playtime from the top 10% and top 1% of players, the typical player (median) and the top 10% player (90th percentile). |

### Retention

Cohorts are the players who arrived on the same day or week. Retention can be filtered by **Campaign**,
**Country**, **Language** and **Edition** (All Java, Premium, Cracked, Bedrock).

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Played again on Day 1 / 7 / 30 | Share of a cohort that came back on day N **or later**. |
| How many new players stay | Share of a cohort active **exactly** on day N (or week N) after arriving. |
| Return by join date | One row per arrival day or week, each cell the share that played again after. More green means more came back. |
| Are new players staying longer? | The Day 1 / 7 / 30 numbers per cohort, over time: is retention getting better? |

A cohort only counts for day N once day N has happened.

<Details title="Comebacks, win-back and what brings players back">
The lower half of the **Full analysis** tab follows every player, not just new ones, and ignores the filters above.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Returned after a week away | An active day whose previous active day was 7 to 29 days earlier. |
| Returned after a month away | An active day whose previous active day was 30 or more days earlier. |
| Share who returned | Players with a comeback ÷ active players. |
| Won back after a week / a month | Of the players who went quiet for 7 (or 30) days in a period, the share that came back within 30 (or 90) days. |
| Returners who stay (comeback stickiness) | Share of comebacks followed by another active day within 7 days. |
| Time between visits | Every gap between visits, bucketed from 1 day to more than 90. |
| What brings players back | Comebacks by the hostname and campaign they came back through. |

Other sections on the same tab: **Players at risk**, **Regulars slipping away**, **Why new players don't come back**,
**What the players who stay did on day one** and **Where they quit**.
</Details>

### Player sources

Open **Player sources**, titled **Where players come from**.

| Section | Shows |
|---|---|
| KPIs | **New players**, **Played 10+ minutes** (activation: share whose first day holds 10 minutes of playtime or more), **Bedrock share** of new players, and **Votes** with the number of voters. |
| Sources and campaigns | New players, cost, revenue and payers of each source or campaign. |
| Join addresses, Countries, Languages, Client versions, Java and Bedrock | New players by the address they typed, where they connect from, their game language, their Minecraft version and their edition, each with its activation and Day 7 retention. |
| Tracked sources, Unknown or direct IP | Sources you named with a campaign, against joins from an address that no campaign claims. |
| First visit | What new players did on their first visit, by source. |
| Votes | Votes per period and which voting sites bring them. Empty without NuVotifier. |

Countries come from the player's IP, looked up on the ingest ("IP Geolocation by DB-IP"). Languages come from the
client's language setting. Client versions come from the protocol number, which a standalone Paper server reads
from ViaVersion when it is installed.

### Campaigns

A **campaign** is a promotion with its own address: give each place you advertise a hostname, such as
`tiktok.yourserver.net`, point it at your proxy, and record what you paid. A player belongs to the campaign of the
hostname of their **first** session, for ever.

- **New campaign** takes a name and one or more hostnames, separated by commas or new lines. Templates prefill the
  name: Discord, YouTube, Ads, Server list, Something else.
- **Record a cost**: an amount on a day, with an optional note. Costs on more than one day make the campaign a
  shaded period on the charts.
- The **Hostnames** table lists every hostname new players used in the range, and maps each to a campaign.
  Hostnames are compared normalised: lowercase, no port, no trailing dot, and the extras that Forge, TCPShield and
  BungeeCord forwarding append are stripped.

Each campaign's own page shows **New players**, **Revenue**, **Players who paid**, **Played 10+ minutes**, **Spend**,
**Cost per new player** (CAC: spend ÷ new players), **Revenue per player** and **Revenue per 1 spent** (ROAS:
store revenue after refunds from the campaign's players ÷ spend, so 2.00× brings back two for each one spent).
Their countries, editions, versions, retention cohorts and top spenders follow; revenue and spenders appear only
for members who can see revenue. The dashboard also defines **Return (ROI)** as (revenue − cost) ÷ cost.

Creating campaigns, mapping hostnames and editing journeys needs **Create campaigns, map hostnames and edit
journeys** (`manage_campaigns`).
