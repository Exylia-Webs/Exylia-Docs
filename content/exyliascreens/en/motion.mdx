You end up with a screen that moves: cards that arrive one after another, a hover that lifts, a purchase that pops and a refusal that shakes. None of it needs Java. Motion is written in CSS, like the rest of the [look](/docs/exyliascreens/styling), plus one action, `animate`, that plays an animation when something is clicked.

Motion is cheap because the **client** does the moving: the server sends where a box should go and how long to take, and the player's game glides it there. A whole screen arriving or a badge pulsing for a minute costs a few dozen small packets, not one per frame.

<Callout type="note" title="Needs ExyliaLib 1.565.0">
`stagger`, `text-reveal`, the `animate` action, the `bounce` and `elastic` easings, animation delays, counts and `alternate`, and the shipped keyframes arrived in ExyliaLib 1.565.0. Arrivals, hover lifts and looping animations are older.
</Callout>

## Before you start

Have a [screen](/docs/exyliascreens/screens) with a list of cards, and a sheet to put the rules in (`<id>.css`, see [Style your screens](/docs/exyliascreens/styling)).

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Lift the card under the cursor">
    ```css
    card { transition: transform 3t ease-out; }
    card:hover { transform: translateY(-3px); }          /* lifts under the cursor */
    card:active { transform: scale(0.96); }              /* pressed for a moment */
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the cards arrive one by one">
    ```css
    .modes { stagger: 4t; }
    .mode { enter: translateY(12px) scale(0.94) 7t back; }
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the click react">
    ```html
    <card id="buy-sword"
          require="balance() >= 1000"
          deny="animate shake; sound entity.villager.no 0.4; toast error 'Not enough coins'"
          action="[MONEY] take 1000; [CONSOLE] give %player_name% diamond_sword; animate pop; toast success 'Bought'">
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check that it works

Open the screen: the cards drop in one after another, a hovered card rises, a purchase pops the card and a click without coins shakes it. A refusal that shakes and a success that pops is the house feel: use it on every card that can say no.

## The five kinds of motion

| What | Written as | Plays |
|---|---|---|
| A change | `transition: transform 3t ease-out;` | when a value changes: a hover, a click, a page |
| An arrival | `enter: translateY(8px) 5t back;` | when a box first shows |
| A departure | `leave: scale(0.94) 3t ease-in;` | when a box goes away |
| A loop or a sequence | `animation: float 60t ease-in-out infinite;` | for as long as the box is shown |
| A reaction | `action="animate pop"` | once, when the action runs |

Each one moves the **transform** of a box: `translate(x, y)`, `translateX`, `translateY` (pixels, y down), `scale(s)` and `scaleX(s)` (narrowing across, which is how a card flips). A box carries everything inside it. Times are ticks (`4t`, or a bare `4`), milliseconds (`200ms`) or seconds (`0.3s`); twenty ticks are one second. Easings: `linear`, `ease-in`, `ease-out` (or `ease`), `ease-in-out`, `back` (goes a little past and settles), `bounce` (lands and bounces twice) and `elastic` (springs and wobbles into place).

## Lists that fill in one by one

`stagger` on a box makes its children arrive one after another instead of all at once. The first card arrives at once, the second 4 ticks later, and so on; each waits shrunk to nothing until its turn, then plays its own `enter`. Rows that only lay things out (a grid's lines) are seen through, so a `grid` with `stagger` fills in cell by cell. The wait stops growing after two seconds, so a long list fills in quickly. Animations start as far apart, which turns a loop into a wave: give the cards of a staggered row `animation: float 60t ease-in-out infinite` and they bob one after another.

## Animations from a click

The `animate` action plays an animation once, on top of whatever the box already does. `animate pop` plays on the clicked element, which needs an `id`. `animate #balance jump` plays on another one. After the name you may add a duration and an easing (`animate pop 8t back`); without them it lasts 10 ticks. Screens only; the action list is in [Add actions and requirements](/docs/exyliascreens/actions-and-requirements#actions).

## Loops with keyframes

```css
.badge { animation: pulse 40t ease-in-out infinite; }
@keyframes pulse { 0% { transform: scale(1); } 50% { transform: scale(1.06); } 100% { transform: scale(1); } }
```

ExyliaLib's base theme ships eight keyframes any screen can name without writing its own: `shake`, `pop`, `pulse`, `float`, `nudge`, `jump`, `flip` and `drop-in`. The classes `.anim-float`, `.anim-pulse` and `.anim-nudge` loop the first three useful ones. A sheet that declares keyframes of the same name uses its own.

<Details title="Animation shorthand and the shipped keyframes">

The full shorthand is `animation: <keyframes> <duration> [easing] [delay] [count | infinite] [alternate]`:

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| The first time | How long one play lasts. |
| A second time | How long it waits before it starts, holding its first frame. |
| A number | How many times it plays (once when left out). |
| `infinite` | Plays for as long as the box is shown. |
| `alternate` | Every second play runs backwards, so a loop swings to and fro. |

`animation-delay`, `animation-iteration-count` and `animation-direction` set the same things one at a time and win over the shorthand.

| Keyframes | Does | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| `shake` | A quick side to side | a refused click |
| `pop` | Swells and settles | a purchase, a claim |
| `pulse` | A gentle swell | something waiting for the player |
| `float` | Rises and sinks | icons, art |
| `nudge` | A small push to the right | an arrow saying "this way" |
| `jump` | A hop and a little one | a notification |
| `flip` | Narrows to its edge and back | a card turning over |
| `drop-in` | Falls a little into place | a single arrival |

</Details>

## Words that type themselves

```css
.headline { text-reveal: 1t; }
```

The words appear one character per tick, after the box has arrived, and again whenever they change (a page turn, a new value). `2t` types at half the speed. It is inherited, so on a box it types all the text inside, and together with `stagger` the lines type one after another:

```css
.words { stagger: 10t; }
.words h2, .words p { text-reveal: 1t; }
```

Keep it for titles and lines of a few words: a paragraph of 90 characters takes four and a half seconds, and a number that changes every second would type out every second.

## What it costs

A transition, an arrival or a departure is one packet per box, two to four for an eased one (eight for `bounce` and `elastic`). A staggered list costs the same, spread over time. A looping animation is one packet per box every 2 to 10 ticks while shown. `animate` is a handful of packets, then nothing, and `text-reveal` one text update every 2 ticks while typing.

ExyliaLib's `screens.yml` `motion-ceiling` caps the motion packets a viewer gets each second; past it, glides go straight and animations wait. A player who turned on **Reduced motion** in `/settings` (or a server with `motion: REDUCED`) gets no arrivals, departures, animations or typing: the screen is simply there.

## The player's frame rate

Motion is nearly free for the player's game; what a screen **shows** is not. Every box, text and item is a display entity the client draws each frame, and a picture (`<img>`, a face, a body, a background image) is the heaviest of all: each of its pixels is a glyph the client lays out four times. On a mid-range card (RTX 3050, 1080p) every shipped example holds the game near 120 frames a second; the daily rewards, the heaviest, keep the card 64% busy, and 40% with Lighter screens on.

- `/screensadmin check` lists every screen that has been heavy for a player since the server started, with its displays and picture glyphs. Fewer or smaller pictures help most.
- A player with a weak computer can turn on **Lighter screens** in `/settings`, Interface: every screen draws its items as 3D items and leaves decoration out, for them only.

## What Minecraft cannot draw

Boxes are flat rectangles drawn by the game itself, with no resource pack. These do **not** exist: rotation, fading in or out, colours that glide, rounded corners, shadows around boxes and blur. A fade is written as a scale from almost nothing (`enter: scale(0.6) 6t back`), and a colour change (a hover) happens in steps.
