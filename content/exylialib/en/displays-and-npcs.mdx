---
title: "Displays and NPCs"
description: "Solid objects, timed effects and player-shaped bodies, sent as packets and animated by the client."
---

None of these creates a real entity. Everything is drawn straight to a client, and each module owns the
lifetime of what it shows.

| Module | Entry point | What it draws |
|---|---|---|
| Displays | `Displays.of(plugin)` | Item, block, head and text displays that move, spin and fall. |
| Vfx | `Vfx.at(location)` | A timeline of displays, beams, sounds, particles and shakes, with ground telegraphs and floating numbers. |
| NPCs | `Npcs.of(plugin)` | Player-shaped bodies: a corpse, a statue, a double, or a placed NPC people click. |
| Ragdolls | `Ragdolls.of(plugin)` | A body cut into its parts and choreographed. See [Ragdolls](/docs/exylialib/ragdolls). |

[Holograms](/docs/exylialib/holograms) and [models worn by mobs](/docs/exylialib/mobs-and-models) are
built on the same displays and have their own pages.

All of them need **PacketEvents**. Without it the module says so once and draws nothing.

<Callout type="note" title="Most callers never touch the API">
Displays, NPCs and ragdolls are all reachable from a [sequence](/docs/exylialib/sequences) line, which is
how every cosmetic plugin in the ecosystem uses them. The API is for what configuration cannot describe.
</Callout>

## Why the client animates it

A display is told a pose and how long it has to get there, and draws every frame in between at the
viewer's own frame rate. A two-second animation is about **six packets per viewer**, and it stays
smooth on a server running at fifteen ticks a second, because the smoothness never depended on the
tick rate.

Moving something by re-sending its position every tick is a twenty-frames-a-second animation that
gets worse under load — which is what a particle trail pretending to be an object looks like.

<Callout type="tip" title="Displays are not a replacement for particles">
Displays give an effect weight, silhouette and shadow. Particles give it light, smoke and atmosphere.
Effects that look expensive are both.
</Callout>

## Drawing a display from a sequence

Any shape line becomes a display line by naming what it is drawn with:

```yaml
effects:
  # Twelve swords that fall out of the sky in a ring, spinning as they come.
  - '[CIRCLE] NETHERITE_SWORD;as:item;radius:2.6;points:12;from:0,9,0;to:0,0,0;spin:2;axis:x;life:0.9;face_out:true;light:15'
  # The floor buckling: a ring of blocks, flattened, growing outwards.
  - '[CIRCLE] CRYING_OBSIDIAN;as:block;radius:0.3;points:20;size:0.1;size_to:1.4;to:0,0.2,0;life:0.5;light:15'
  # One item, thrown up, tumbling, falling back down.
  - '[DISPLAY] TRIDENT;from:0,0.5,0;to:0,4,0;gravity:14;spin:3;axis:z;life:1.4;size:1.5'
  # A line of text that rides with whoever set it off, on a dark panel.
  - '[DISPLAY] {primary}&lCRITICAL;as:text;rise:0.6;life:1.2;ease:back_out;follow:true;background:#000000;background_alpha:96'
```

`as:` says what the line's first word is:

| `as:` | The first word is |
|---|---|
| `item` | A material, or `{item:name}` for an item the plugin hands over when it plays. The default on `[DISPLAY]`. |
| `block` | A material or a full block state: `oak_stairs[facing=north]`. |
| `head` | A texture, `{victim}` or `{killer}`. |
| `text` | A line of text; `%placeholders%` are filled on every play. |

### How it moves

| Parameter | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `life:` | Seconds it exists; `1500ms` also works. | `1` |
| `from:x,y,z` | Where it starts, relative to its point. | `0,0,0` |
| `to:x,y,z` | Where it ends. | `0,0,0` |
| `rise:` | Shorthand for `to:0,n,0`. | |
| `gravity:` | Blocks per second squared, **added** to the line. | `0`; vanilla is about `32` |
| `ease:` | The curve, below. An unknown name is linear. | `linear` |
| `spin:` | Turns over its whole life; `x,y,z` tumbles on three axes. | `0` |
| `axis:` | Which axis a single-number spin turns around. | `y` |
| `orbit:` | Turns each point carries round the anchor. | `0` |
| `vary:` | How much the pieces differ in size, as a fraction. | `0` |
| `size:` | One number, or `x,y,z` for a plate, a pillar or a blade. | `1` |
| `size_to:` | The size it ends at. | same as `size` |
| `tilt:` `roll:` `turn:` | A fixed rotation, in degrees. | `0` |
| `face_out:` | Each point faces away from the centre. | `false` |
| `pull:` | How far each point travels towards the centre; negative throws it outwards. | `0` |
| `follow:` | `true` seats it on whoever set the sequence off, so it goes where they go; `victim` on the victim. | stays put |
| `glow:` | Outline colour: a name, `#rrggbb` or a `{palette}` token. | none |
| `light:` | Fixed light level, `0` to `15`. | the light where it stands |
| `model:` | Custom model data, for items and heads. | none |
| `billboard:` | `FIXED`, `VERTICAL`, `HORIZONTAL`, `CENTER`. | `FIXED`, `CENTER` for text |
| `hold:` | Item display context, `0` to `8`: `0` the model, `5` head, `7` dropped, `8` item frame. | `0` |

`orbit:` is the movement a straight line cannot express and the one that separates a shape that
appears from a shape that is alive. `vary:0.4` gives the pieces sizes that differ by up to two fifths
— a hash of each point's position, not a random number, so it is the same every play. `spin:0.5,2,1`
tumbles on three axes, because nothing thrown in the world spins about exactly one.

### Easing

`linear`, `in`, `out` and `in_out` are the plain curves. `back` overshoots and settles, `anticipate`
pulls back before it goes, `elastic` springs, `bounce` lands and bounces, `snap` cuts straight to the end,
`smooth` passes through every pose without a corner, and `hold` stays put until the end. The families
`sine`, `quad`, `cubic`, `quart`, `quint`, `expo`, `circ`, `back`, `elastic` and `bounce` each take
`_in`, `_out` or `_in_out`: `back_out`, `quart_in_out`. `easeOutQuart` is read too.

### Text lines

| Parameter | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `background:` | Panel colour, or `none`. | no panel |
| `background_alpha:` | The panel's alpha, `0` to `255`. | `64` |
| `opacity:` | Text opacity, `0` to `255`. | opaque |
| `see_through:` | Shows through walls. | `true` |
| `text_shadow:` | Chat-style drop shadow. | `false` |
| `align:` | `left`, `center` or `right`. | `center` |
| `line_width:` | Wrap width, in pixels. | `200` |
| `view_range:` | A multiple of the client's entity distance. Any kind of line. | `1` |
| `cull:` | Culling box, `cull:4` or `cull:4,3`, for a wide piece that vanishes at the edge of the screen. Any kind of line. | the client's |

<Callout type="note" title="Text displays draw no panel">
A text display has no background unless one is set. Before 1.311 it kept the client's grey panel;
an effect that relied on it now wants `background:#000000;background_alpha:64`.
</Callout>

### Making a movement land

<Steps>
  <Step title="ease:in for a slam">
    It holds the movement back and spends it late: a wind-up and a strike, from the same two numbers.
    `back_out` is the opposite: it arrives early, overshoots and settles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="gravity: for debris only">
    It is added to the line rather than replacing it, so `from:0,9,0;to:0,1,0;gravity:40` descends
    eight blocks **and then** falls twelve more, through the floor. Half of `gravity` times the life
    squared is how far it drops: a second in the air wants about `5`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="size: for weight">
    `size:3,0.15,3` is a block flattened into a plate. Grown from nothing it is a shockwave;
    stretched the other way it is a pillar or a blade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="size_to: for arrival">
    A few percent larger on impact, or smaller on a wind-up. It is the difference between an object
    arriving and an object being placed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="A blade is aimed with roll:, not tilt:">
An item model is a flat plate standing in its own XY plane, facing south, with a sword's tip up and
to the right. `roll:135` puts it tip-down, `roll:315` tip-up, `roll:225` point-first along its face.
The turn runs the opposite way from the right-hand rule, so a blade that comes out horizontal wants
90 added or taken. `face_out:` and `turn:` are applied **after** `roll:` and `tilt:`, which is the
order a ring needs. A block display is a cube and needs none of it.
</Callout>

`light:15` is worth setting on nearly every effect: a display lit by the world is black at night, and
an effect that disappears after sunset is an effect players report as broken.

## The display API

```java
PluginDisplays displays = Displays.of(this);

DisplayModel blade = DisplayModel.item(new ItemStack(Material.NETHERITE_SWORD))
        .glow(0xFF6B9D)
        .light(15);

DisplayMotion thrown = DisplayMotion.builder()
        .life(1200)
        .from(0, 7, 0).to(0, 0, 0)
        .spin(Rotation.Axis.Z, 3)
        .ease(DisplayMotion.Easing.BACK_OUT)
        .build();

displays.show(blade, thrown, where, observers);
```

| `PluginDisplays` | What it does |
|---|---|
| `show(model, motion, at, viewers)` | Shows one display to a fixed list; it removes itself when the motion ends. Returns a `DisplayHandle`, or `null` when nobody can see it or PacketEvents is absent. |
| `show(model, motion, at, radius)` | A **tracked** audience: who is in range is read again every half second, and a player who walks up is sent it mid-flight. |
| `show(model, motion, at, radius, who)` | Tracked, with a filter. |
| `rideOn(model, motion, entity, viewers)` | Seated on an entity; the client carries it. |
| `removeAll()` / `active()` / `isSupported()` | Everything this plugin shows, how many, whether it can. |

`DisplayModel` is `item`, `block`, `text(component)` or `text(player -> component)` for a line that differs
per viewer, then `glow`, `light`, `billboard`, `held`, and for text `background`, `opacity`,
`seeThrough`, `textShadow`, `align` and `lineWidth`. `shadow(radius, strength)` gives any display a
ground shadow, and exists only in the API.

### Motions

| `DisplayMotion` | What it is |
|---|---|
| `builder()` | `life`, `from`, `to`, `scale`, `rotation`, `spin`, `gravity`, `ease`, and `easePosition`, `easeRotation`, `easeScale` for one curve each; `turnTo(rotation)` turns towards a pose as it goes. |
| `chain(a, b, c…)` | Motions back to back, each with its own easing: a rise, a hang, a drop. |
| `between(from, to, millis, easing)` | A glide between two keyframes, the short way round. |
| `spline(keyframes, life)` | A smooth curve through every keyframe. |
| `of(keyframes, life)` / `still(life)` | Exact poses, or none. |
| `looping(cycle, accel, maxSpeed)` | Repeats, optionally speeding up each cycle. |

### Changing a display while it plays

Every call on a `DisplayHandle` is safe from any thread.

| `DisplayHandle` | What it does |
|---|---|
| `showing(item)`, `text(…)`, `glow(rgb)`, `light(level)`, `restyle(model)` | Changes how it looks; the motion carries on untouched. |
| `pose()` | Where it is on screen now. |
| `then(motion)` | Plays a new motion from there. |
| `moveTo(keyframe, millis, easing)` | Glides to a pose, keeping any longer life it had left. |
| `speedUp(factor)` | Plays the rest faster. |
| `settle(millis)` | Eases back to where it started, then goes. |
| `remove()` / `isShowing()` | Lifetime. |

```java
DisplayHandle reel = displays.show(DisplayModel.item(icon),
        DisplayMotion.builder().life(10_000).spin(Rotation.Axis.Z, 40).build(), at, 24);

reel.showing(nextIcon);       // swap the item, the spin carries on
reel.speedUp(2.0);            // the reel slows down by being told to stop sooner
```

## Vfx, telegraphs and indicators

A `Vfx` is one effect written as a timeline: what happens at which millisecond, played as a unit and
cancelled as a unit. It is how a boss's slam, a crate opening or a lottery draw is built in code.

```java
Vfx slam = Vfx.at(centre).track(48);
Telegraphs.circle(slam, 0, centre, 5, 900, 0xFF9500);       // fills for 0.9 s: the dodge window

DisplayMotion rubble = DisplayMotion.chain(
        DisplayMotion.builder().life(150).to(0, 0.6, 0).ease(DisplayMotion.Easing.OUT).build(),
        DisplayMotion.builder().life(300).from(0, 0.6, 0).to(0, -0.2, 0).ease(DisplayMotion.Easing.BOUNCE_OUT).build());

slam.sound(900, Sound.ENTITY_GENERIC_EXPLODE, 0.8f, 0.6f)
    .display(900, DisplayModel.block(Material.DEEPSLATE.createBlockData()).light(15), rubble, centre)
    .shake(900, 10, 2, 50);

VfxRun run = slam.play(plugin);
run.cancel();                 // the caster died mid wind-up
```

| `Vfx` | What it does |
|---|---|
| `at(location)` | Starts one. |
| `viewers(players)` / `nearby(radius)` / `track(radius)` | Who sees it: a list, everyone in range when it is built, or everyone in range while it plays. |
| `lod(true)` | Lower detail; turned on by itself past fifteen viewers. |
| `display(at, model, motion)` / `ride(at, model, motion, entity)` | A display. |
| `beam(at, from, to, model, thickness, life)` | A beam between two points. |
| `sound`, `particle`, `firework` | What they say; the firework is a packet, not an entity. |
| `lines(at, sequence, target)` | Plays a compiled sequence. |
| `shake(at, radius, times, every)` | Shakes the camera of players in range. |
| `call(at, runnable)` | Runs code at that moment. |
| `play(plugin)` | Plays it and returns a `VfxRun`: `cancel()`, `speedUp(factor)`, `isDone()`, `viewers()`. |

The whole effect counts against `max-per-effect`; what goes over is dropped and counted in `dropped()`.

**Ground telegraphs** show where something will land. They are glass plates laid flat on the ground,
lit, and their fill doubles as the timer:

| `Telegraphs` | Shape |
|---|---|
| `circle(vfx, at, centre, radius, fillMillis, rgb)` | A disc that fills outwards. |
| `ring(…)` | A ring that closes inwards. |
| `cone(vfx, at, apex, yaw, radius, degrees, fillMillis, rgb)` | A cone, for a cleave. |
| `line(vfx, at, from, to, width, fillMillis, rgb)` | A strip, for a charge or a fissure. |
| `cross(vfx, at, centre, length, fillMillis, rgb)` | A cross. |

**Floating indicators** are the numbers that pop off something hit:

```java
Indicators numbers = Indicators.of(plugin).range(32);
numbers.damage(entity, finalDamage, critical);
numbers.heal(player, 4);
numbers.text(entity, Text.component("{warning}Blocked"));
```

Hits of the same kind within 0.3 s merge into one summed number, at most four float over one entity, and
the default range is 24 blocks. Damage is drawn in `{error}`, a critical in `{warning}` with a ✦, and a heal in `{success}` as `+4 ❤`.
Call them from the entity's own thread.

## NPCs

### From a sequence

```yaml
effects:
  # The victim, face down, wearing what they died in, for four seconds.
  - '[NPC] {victim};pose:lying;life:4;equip:true'
  # Standing, turned to face whoever did it, outlined.
  - '[NPC] {victim};pose:standing;life:3;glow:{error};face:true'
```

| Parameter | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `pose:` | `lying`, `standing`, `crawling`, `sneaking` or `spinning`. An unknown pose stands. | `lying` |
| `life:` | Seconds it stays, from `0.2` to `120`. | `5` |
| `equip:` | Wears the armour and weapon they have on. | `true` |
| `glow:` | Outline colour. | none |
| `y:` | Height above the anchor. | `0` |
| `face:` | Turns to face whoever set the sequence off. | `true` |
| `from:x,y,z` / `to:x,y,z` | Where it appears and where it ends up. | `0,0,0` |
| `over:` | Seconds the movement takes. | `0.7` |
| `move_after:` | Seconds before the movement or flinch starts. | `0` |
| `ease:` | `out`, `in`, `in_out` or `linear`. | `out` |
| `gravity:` | Blocks per second squared, added to the line. | `0` |
| `turn:` | Degrees it turns on the spot over the movement. | `0` |
| `pose_to:` / `after:` | A second pose, and how long before it. | none / `0.4` |
| `hurt:` | Flinches red as it appears. | `false` |
| `spin:` | Degrees a second it keeps turning, for its whole life. | `0` |
| `bob:` / `bob_every:` | Blocks it rises and falls on a loop, and how long one rise and fall takes. | `0` / `1.6` |
| `swing:` | Seconds between arm swings. | `0` |
| `scale:` | How big it is drawn, `1` being player-sized. | `1` |
| `hold:` / `offhand:` | A material put in each hand. | its own |
| `pitch:` | Head pitch in degrees, negative being up. | `0` |

`spin:` is the one to reach for when a body should not settle: `turn:` is one turn spread over the
movement and then finished, `spin:` never stops. A body caught in a vortex is `spin:220`, one hanging
in the light is `bob:0.3;spin:40`, and one still swinging at whatever killed it is
`swing:0.5;hold:NETHERITE_SWORD`. `scale:` is the client's own scale attribute (`0.0625` to `16`), so the
whole model grows; it needs a 1.20.5 client, and an older one sees a player-sized body.

A body reads better at about **half vanilla gravity**. `pose:lying` is the pose a sleeping player is drawn
in, which is the only way to put a body on the floor without a model of your own.

<Callout type="note" title="Why an NPC cannot take a player name">
`{victim}` and `{killer}` read the texture from the connection this server already holds — no lookup,
no waiting, no failure halfway. A name would be a request to Mojang, and an effect cannot wait for one,
so the line refuses it. A base64 texture is accepted, but it carries no signature, and a modern client
draws an unsigned texture as the default skin.
</Callout>

### From code

```java
PluginNpcs npcs = Npcs.of(this);

NpcHandle body = npcs.show(NpcModel.of(victim)
                .wearing(victim)
                .pose(NpcPose.LYING)
                .glow(0xA33B53),
        victim.getLocation(), 4000, observers);

body.lookAt(killer.getLocation());
```

`NpcModel` is `of(player)`, `of(name, texture, signature)` or `of(name)` for the default skin, then
`pose`, `glow`, `wearing`, `holding`, `offHand` and `scale`. An `NpcMotion` passed to `show` describes the
same movement as the line. The `NpcHandle` can `look`, `lookAt`, `pose`, `moveTo` (a short hop glides,
a long one jumps), `teleportTo`, `using(true)` to raise a bow or shield, `equip`, `swing`, `hurt` and
`remove`.

<Callout type="warning" title="An NPC always has its own identity">
It is announced under a UUID of its own, never the wearer's: a second entry under a real player's id
takes that player's skin off their own body until they relog. It is also announced **unlisted**, so it
never appears in the tab list beside real players.
</Callout>

### Placed NPCs

A placed NPC stays where it is put until it is removed: a shopkeeper, a quest giver, a lobby guide. It is
drawn for every player in range and taken away as they leave.

```java
PlacedNpc guide = npcs.place("guide", NpcSpec.builder()
        .skin(NpcSkin.player("Notch"))
        .look(NpcLook.EACH_VIEWER)
        .lookRange(10)
        .equip(EquipmentSlot.HAND, new ItemStack(Material.COMPASS))
        .animations(animations)                  // read from config, below
        .build(), location);

guide.visibleIf(player -> player.hasPermission("lobby.guide"));
guide.onClick((player, click) -> menus.open(player, "guide"));
```

| `NpcSpec` | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `skin` | `NpcSkin.none()`, `player(name)`, `texture(value, signature)`, or `mirror()`: each viewer sees their own skin. | none |
| `pose` | `STANDING`, `SNEAKING`, `LYING`… | `STANDING` |
| `scale` | `0.0625` to `16`. | `1` |
| `glow` | Outline, drawn in the nearest of the sixteen team colours. | none |
| `equip(slot, item)` | What it wears and holds. | nothing |
| `look` | `FIXED` faces its location's way; `NEAREST` turns one head to the nearest player; `EACH_VIEWER` turns to each player on their own screen. | `FIXED` |
| `lookRange` | How close a player must be to be looked at. | `8` |
| `viewDistance` | Blocks; past this it is not drawn. | `48` |
| `sitting` | Sits on an invisible seat, hips at the location. | `false` |
| `animations` | Click, idle and greeting animations. | none |

- **Range.** Who sees it is worked out off the main thread four times a second. `visibleIf` is checked on
  every pass and must read memory only.
- **Skins.** `player(name)` reads an online player's skin from their connection, and otherwise asks Mojang
  off the main thread, once a run. The NPC wears the default skin until it arrives and is redrawn then.
  A `texture` without its signature is drawn as the default skin.
- **The head.** With nobody in range a turned head eases back home over about a second.
- **No name, no tab entry.** The nametag is always hidden and collision is off. Once drawn it leaves the
  player list, so commands stop completing it.
- **Clicks.** Only a player who has it drawn can click it, and a second click within 200 ms is dropped.
  `onClick` runs on that player's thread with a `Click`.

`PlacedNpc` also has `moveTo`, `update(spec)`, `swing`, `hurt`, `play(animation)`, `perform(performance)`,
`isViewing`, `viewerCount` and `remove`; `npcs.placed(id)`, `allPlaced()` and `unplace(id)` find them.

### Animations

`NpcAnimations` is a configuration section, so server owners set them per NPC:

```yaml
animations:
  left-click: 'flinch'
  right-click: 'wave'
  idle: 'nod'
  idle-every: 20s
  greet: 'wave'
  greet-range: 4.0
  greet-cooldown: 30s
  perform-range: 24.0
```

| Key | When it plays | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `left-click` / `right-click` | On a click, for everyone who sees it, before the click handler. | none |
| `idle` / `idle-every` | On a timer, only while somebody sees it. `0` never. | none / `20s` |
| `greet` | For a player who walks up, seen **only by that player**. | none |
| `greet-range` / `greet-cooldown` | How close, and how long before the same player is greeted again. | `4` / `30s` |
| `perform-range` | How close a player must be to be shown a performance. | `24` |

A value is a preset — `wave`, `wave-offhand`, `nod`, `bow`, `flinch`, `punch`, `cheer` — or moves
joined by commas, with waits between them: `swing, 250ms, offhand, 1s, crouch, 500ms, stand`. The moves
are `swing`, `offhand`, `hurt`, `crouch` and `stand`; an animation that crouched always ends standing.
They are vanilla packets and need no resource pack.

### Ragdoll performances

A value with a `:` is a **performance**: the NPC is hidden and a [ragdoll](/docs/exylialib/ragdolls)
wearing its skin dances in its place, then the NPC comes back. It keeps its hitbox the whole time, so it
can still be clicked.

| Value | What plays |
|---|---|
| `anim:wave` | `plugins/<YourPlugin>/animations/wave.json`. |
| `emote:<id>` | An emote the plugin resolves with `npcs.emotes(id -> …)`. |
| `follow:1;keys:…` | `[RAGDOLL]` options ending in `keys:`. |

Unwritten options default to `intact:0;follow:1;breathe:1`. One performance plays at a time; a loop stops
after eight seconds unless told otherwise. A performance is skipped while the NPC sits, when nobody is
within `perform-range`, or when its emote is unknown. Moving or changing the NPC ends it.

### Clicks as configuration: `Interactions`

Placed NPCs and holograms hand a click over as a `Click` — `LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `SHIFT_LEFT` or
`SHIFT_RIGHT`. `Interactions` turns a list of configuration lines into what that click does:

```java
Interaction onGuide = Interactions.compile(this, config.guideClick(), "npc guide");
guide.onClick((player, click) -> onGuide.run(player, click, Map.of("npc", "guide")));
```

```yaml
guide-click:
  - 'right:[SOUND] UI_BUTTON_CLICK;0.6;1.4'
  - 'right:menu open guide'
  - 'shift_left:console: warp %player_name% spawn'
  - 'left:[MESSAGE] {error}Hey, that hurts.'
```

A line may start with a filter — `left:`, `right:`, `shift_left:`, `shift_right:`, `shift:`, `any:`, or
several as `left,right:` — and no filter means every click. Then `[DELAY] 1s`, `[SERVER] name`,
`[TELEPORT] world;x;y;z`, `[ACTION] ns:id`, any other `[TOKEN]` played as a sequence line for the
clicker, or a command: bare or `player:` as the player, `console:`, `player-proxy:` or `console-proxy:`.
`%placeholders%` resolve for the clicker, from the `data` map first. A step that is refused stops the
lines after it. `Interactions.token(plugin, name, token)` adds a token of the plugin's own.

## Ragdolls

A body cut into its six parts and moved as choreography: thrown apart, opened out, spelled into letters,
danced frame by frame or imported from Blockbench and Emotecraft. They have their own page:
[Ragdolls](/docs/exylialib/ragdolls).

### Real skins: `ragdoll-skin-quality`

Ragdolls wear the player's real skin through Exylia Skins, with no key and no account. The setting and how
it works are on the [Ragdolls](/docs/exylialib/ragdolls#real-skins-ragdoll-skin-quality) page.

## What they guarantee

| | |
|---|---|
| **Nothing the server carries** | Not entities: not ticked, not saved, not in any chunk, no hitbox. Two players standing together can be shown different ones. |
| **Nothing can be left behind** | Nothing else would clean them up, so the module owns their lives: they go when the motion or life ends, when the plugin is disabled, or when the server stops. |
| **One timer, not one per object** | Every display on the server is moved from a single asynchronous driver. |
| **A spin is cut up for you** | The client turns by the shortest arc, so spins are split into sixths of a turn; `spin:3` is correct without anybody knowing that. |
| **Capped work** | A short motion is capped at forty-eight poses, a long one at one pose a tick up to 256, rather than sending whatever a file asks for. |
| **PacketEvents or nothing** | There is no fallback worth pretending about. |

## The ceiling on what displays may cost

`plugins/ExyliaLib/displays.yml` holds three numbers for the whole server, because what a client can be
sent is a fact about the server rather than about the plugin that happened to send it.

```yaml title="plugins/ExyliaLib/displays.yml"
max-viewer-displays: 20000
max-per-effect: 128
max-model-parts: 30000
```

| Key | What it caps |
|---|---|
| `max-viewer-displays` | Display-**viewer pairs** at once: one display seen by thirty players counts as thirty. Effects that would go over are dropped before their packets are built, so a crowded arena loses the tail of an effect instead of its tick rate. The console says so at most once a minute. `0` removes the ceiling. |
| `max-per-effect` | How many displays one shape or one `Vfx` may draw — a `points:900` written by hand is caught at load rather than on the wire. |
| `max-model-parts` | [Model](/docs/exylialib/mobs-and-models) cubes times viewers. Past three quarters of it, models are drawn in less detail; nothing is refused. `0` removes the ceiling. |

The budget counts viewers because that is the number that reaches the network: two hundred displays in
an empty corner are nothing, and the same two hundred with thirty players around them are six thousand
entities on clients that also have a fight to render. `/exylialib reload` picks up an edit; no restart.

## Region flags

WorldGuard flags the library registers, so a region can refuse effects without any plugin depending on
WorldGuard:

| Flag | Denying it stops |
|---|---|
| `kill-effects` | Kill effects playing where somebody died inside the region. |
| `hit-effects` | Hit effects playing on a blow landed inside the region. |
| `arrows-effects` | Arrow launch, trail and impact effects inside the region. |

They are registered from the library's own `onLoad`, because WorldGuard locks its registry the instant
it enables. On a server without WorldGuard every flag allows, and an unregistered flag, a missing world
or a refused registry all answer the same way: a gate that cannot be asked never withholds anything.
