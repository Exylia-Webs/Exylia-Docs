---
title: "Duets"
description: "Emotes that take two: the invitation, the reach, and how the two bodies meet in the middle."
---

A **duet** is an emote with `kind: duet` in `emotes.yml`. Two ship, both in the Social tab:
`highfive` and `handshake`. Both players perform the same choreography at the same moment, each from
their own feet — two people facing each other who both reach forward meet in the middle.

## Inviting

```text
/emote play <emote> <player>
```

Naming a player for a solo emote is quietly ignored and the emote simply plays. Leaving the player out
of a duet is refused with `needs-partner`.

<Callout type="warning" title="The shipped needs-partner line names the wrong command">
Its default reads `/emote %id% <player>`, which only opens the menu. The command is
`/emote play <id> <player>`: change the line in `messages.yml` to `/emote play %id% <player>`.
</Callout>

The invitation is refused on the spot when:

| Refusal | Message |
|---|---|
| You named yourself | `invite-self` |
| They are performing an emote | `partner-busy` |
| They are further than `duet.max-distance`, or in another world | `partner-too-far` |

Otherwise the inviter is told `invite-sent`, the invited player `invite-received` with **Accept** and
**Decline** buttons, and `feedback.on-invite` is played to them.

<Callout type="note" title="Ownership is checked when it starts, not when it is sent">
An invitation is sent even if either player does not own the emote, is on cooldown, in combat or in a
world where emotes are off. Those are all checked on accept, and refuse it then.
</Callout>

## One standing invitation

Each player holds **one** invitation at a time, the one they were sent last. A second replaces the
first, and whoever sent the first is not told. An invitation lasts `duet.invite` — 20 seconds by
default, never less than one — and is swept once a second after that.

## Answering

| To | How |
|---|---|
| Accept | Click **Accept**, run `/emote accept` (alias `yes`), or right-click whoever invited you. |
| Decline | Click **Decline** or run `/emote deny` (aliases `decline`, `no`). The inviter is told `invite-declined`. |

The name after `accept` or `deny` is accepted and ignored: there is only ever one invitation to answer.

Right-clicking the inviter runs `/emote accept <name>` as the player, exactly as if they had typed it —
so whatever stops them typing it (a missing permission, a jail, a combat plugin blocking commands)
stops the right-click too. Right-clicking anybody else is left alone.

| Answer | Message |
|---|---|
| Nothing to accept or decline | `invite-none` |
| It ran out, or the inviter left | `invite-expired` |
| The emote was removed by a reload in between | `unknown-emote` |

## Starting

On accept, in this order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The inviter must be able to play it">
    Owning it, not already performing, in an allowed world, out of combat, off cooldown. The first
    that fails is the answer both of them are told.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The partner must be free">
    Still online and not performing anything, or `partner-busy`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The partner must be able to play it">
    The same checks as the inviter. Any failure is reported as `partner-cannot`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They must still be close">
    Within `duet.max-distance` of each other, in the same world, or `partner-too-far`: between the
    ask and the answer one of them can walk away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Then both bodies start on the same tick, both players are told `playing`, and both cooldowns start for
both of them.

## Where the bodies stand

Not where the players do. Both bodies are placed on the line between the two players, `gap` blocks
apart and turned towards each other, so the choreography meets in the middle whether they were
standing shoulder to shoulder or three blocks apart. Each body keeps its own player's height, so two
players on different steps are not sunk into one of them.

`gap` is part of the emote, not a preference: a handshake is written for arms reaching forward and a
high five for arms reaching up, and the two meet at different distances. The shipped handshake uses
`1.51`, the high five `1.13`. A duet that writes its own frames writes its own gap with them.

Only the bodies move. The players are never teleported into position — their hidden hitboxes stay
exactly where they were standing.

## Ending

The two halves are one emote: anything that ends it for one ends it for both, including a punch at
either body.

<Callout type="note" title="Duets never appear in a showcase">
A showcase plays one body at one spot, and half a handshake is a body reaching out at nothing. They
are left out of every showcase whatever `showcase.emotes` lists.
</Callout>
