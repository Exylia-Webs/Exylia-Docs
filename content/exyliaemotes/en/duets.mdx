You end up with two players performing one emote at the same moment, facing each other. A **duet** is an emote with `kind: duet` in `emotes.yml`. Two ship, both in the Social tab: `highfive` (HIGH FIVE) and `handshake` (HANDSHAKE).

## Before you start

Both players need to own the emote, see [Permissions](/docs/exyliaemotes/permissions). They must stand within `duet.max-distance` blocks of each other (three by default), in the same world. A duet cannot be started from the menu, because a menu row has nobody to name.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite">
    The first player runs `/emote play handshake <player>`, naming the other.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer">
    The invited player clicks **Accept** in the chat message, or runs `/emote accept` (alias `yes`), or right-clicks whoever invited them. To refuse, they click **Decline** or run `/emote deny` (aliases `decline`, `no`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it start">
    Both bodies start on the same tick, both players are told the emote is playing, and the cooldown starts for both.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check that it works

With two players close together, the first runs `/emote play handshake <the other player>`. The other sees a message with **Accept** and **Decline**. After accepting, both vanish into their own cameras and the two bodies meet in the middle. If the invitation is refused, see the reasons below.

## Going further

### Invitations last 20 seconds

Each player holds one invitation at a time, the one they were sent last. A second invitation replaces the first, and whoever sent the first is not told. An invitation lasts `duet.invite`, 20 seconds by default and never less than one.

### Where the bodies stand

Not where the players do. Both bodies are placed on the line between the two players, `gap` blocks apart and turned towards each other, so the choreography meets in the middle whether they stood shoulder to shoulder or three blocks apart. Each body keeps its own player's height. The players are never teleported: their hidden hitboxes stay where they were. The shipped handshake uses a `gap` of `1.51`, the high five `1.13`.

### Ending a duet

The two halves are one emote: anything that ends it for one ends it for both, including a punch at either body.

<Callout type="note" title="Duets never appear in a showcase">
A showcase plays one body at one spot, and half a handshake is a body reaching out at nothing. Duets are left out of every showcase whatever `showcase.emotes` lists. See [Showcases](/docs/exyliaemotes/showcases).
</Callout>

<Details title="Why an invitation is refused, and the message keys">
Naming a player for a solo emote is ignored and the emote simply plays. Leaving the player out of a duet is refused with `needs-partner`, which tells the player to use `/emote play %id% <player>`. Servers that ran 1.3 or older had it naming `/emote %id% <player>`, which only opens the menu; updating rewrites that part of the line and keeps the rest.

The invitation is refused on the spot when:

| Refusal | Message key |
|---|---|
| You named yourself | `invite-self` |
| They are performing an emote | `partner-busy` |
| They are further than `duet.max-distance`, or in another world | `partner-too-far` |

Otherwise the inviter gets `invite-sent`, the invited player `invite-received` with the buttons, and `feedback.on-invite` is played to them. An invitation is sent even if a player does not own the emote, is on cooldown, in combat or in a world where emotes are off: those are checked when it is accepted.

Right-clicking the inviter runs `/emote accept <name>` as the player, as if they had typed it, so whatever stops them typing it (a missing permission, a combat plugin blocking commands) stops the right-click too. The name after `accept` or `deny` is ignored: there is only ever one invitation to answer.

| Answer | Message key |
|---|---|
| Nothing to accept or decline | `invite-none` |
| It ran out, or the inviter left | `invite-expired` |
| The emote was removed by a reload in between | `unknown-emote` |
| The invited player declined | `invite-declined` (told to the inviter) |

On accept, these are checked in order:

1. The inviter owns the emote, is not already performing, is in an allowed world, out of combat and off cooldown. The first failure is told to both.
2. The partner is still online and not performing anything, or `partner-busy`.
3. The partner passes the same checks as the inviter, or `partner-cannot`.
4. They are still within `duet.max-distance` in the same world, or `partner-too-far`.
</Details>
