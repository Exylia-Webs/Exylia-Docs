---
title: "The crate"
description: "Rarities, both kinds of key, the reels that fall, and the blocks that open them."
---

The crate is how emotes are won rather than given. A key is spent, a reel falls, and what it lands on
is unlocked on the player's account — owned for good, whatever happens to their rank afterwards. The
crate itself is ExyliaLib's; this plugin hands it the catalogue and reads its settings and lines from
`config.yml` and `messages.yml`.

## Rarities

Every emote declares a `tier` in `emotes.yml`, and `config.yml` declares what the tiers are:

```yaml
tiers:
  common:
    name: "Common"
    material: GRAY_DYE
    color: "{muted}"
    chance: 60.0
    priority: 1
  rare:
    name: "Rare"
    material: LIGHT_BLUE_DYE
    color: "{info}"
    chance: 25.0
    priority: 2
  epic:
    name: "Epic"
    material: PINK_DYE
    color: "{accent}"
    chance: 12.0
    priority: 3
  legendary:
    name: "Legendary"
    material: ORANGE_DYE
    color: "{highlight}"
    chance: 3.0
    priority: 4
```

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| `name` | What menus and placeholders call it. |
| `material` | The item the rarity is drawn with. |
| `color` | The colour its name and its emotes are written in. |
| `chance` | How often a crate lands on it, **relative to the other rarities**. |
| `priority` | Where it sits in menus; lower comes first. |

The shipped catalogue has 20 common, 22 rare, 19 epic and 7 legendary emotes.

Nothing here is hard-coded. Delete `legendary`, invent `mythic`, rename all four: the key is the id
`emotes.yml` writes as `tier`, and an emote naming a rarity that no longer exists falls back to the
first one — it loses its colour, never its existence.

<Callout type="note" title="chance is a weight, not a percentage">
The shares are worked out from the weights, so four rarities weighted `60/25/12/3` and four weighted
`6/2.5/1.2/0.3` behave identically. Deleting a rarity re-spreads its odds across the rest instead of
leaving a three-percent hole.
</Callout>

## Keys

Two kinds, and the crate takes both.

### On the account

A number on the player's crate row. Nothing can be dropped, duped, or lost to a full inventory:

```text
/emotesadmin keys give <player> <amount>
/emotesadmin keys take <player> <amount>
/emotesadmin keys set <player> <amount>
/emotesadmin keys check <player>
```

Safe to run from console and for players who are offline — a shop, a vote listener or a reward plugin
hands keys over the same way:

```text
[console] emotesadmin keys give %player% 1
```

Every player is given `crate.start-keys` keys — one by default — the first time they are seen.

### In the hand

An item, so it can be sold, put in a kit, dropped and given to a friend:

```text
/emotesadmin item key <player> [1-64]
```

Right click a bound crate block holding one and it opens on the spot: one key, one reel, no question
screen. Left clicking never spends one — that is how a player asks what a block is. How the key is
drawn is `key-item` in `config.yml`; it is recognised by what it carries in its data, not by its name or
material. Keys that do not fit in the inventory wait and are handed over on the player's next join,
with `rewards-claimed`.

Key items minted by the versions of this plugin that ran their own crate keep opening it.

## Opening

`/emote crate`, the **EMOTE CRATE** button in the menu, or a bound block clicked without a key in hand.
The first screen asks only one thing: how many at once.

<Steps>
  <Step title="How many">
    `menus/crate.yml`. Choices are drawn up to `crate.max-at-once`, capped at seven by the width of
    the screen, and spread the way the reels will be — where you click is where that reel falls. A
    choice the player cannot afford is drawn by `empty_template` with how many keys it is missing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The reels fall">
    `menus/crate_open.yml`. One reel per crate, each running the full height of the chest, each with
    its own finishing line `crate.stagger-seconds` after the one before it. Every face keeps its name
    and lore the whole way down.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What went past stays">
    Nothing is cleared when a reel stops. What it came within one slot of stays on screen, because
    half of what a crate is worth is the legendary that went past the line.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paid out as each reel lands">
    Keys are spent and prizes decided before the first frame is drawn. Each reel pays out as it
    stops, with `crate.on-win` or `crate.on-duplicate` and its own line in chat. Closing the window
    mid-fall costs nothing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Refusal | Message |
|---|---|
| `crate.enabled` is off | `crate-disabled` |
| Not enough keys | `crate-no-keys` — `%amount%` is the cost, `%keys%` what they hold. |
| Their last opening is still falling | `crate-busy` |
| The catalogue is empty | `crate-empty` |

## What comes out

An emote is unlocked on the account. `crate.reward` accepts `UNLOCK`, `ITEM` and `BOTH`, but an emote
has no item to hand over, so all three behave as `UNLOCK`: a key never buys nothing. A server that wants
no crate at all sets `crate.enabled: false`.

`crate-won` names what came out (`%emote%` or `%reward%`) and its rarity (`%tier%`).

## Duplicates

Landing on something the player has already unlocked hands keys back, as many as
`crate.duplicate-refund` says, with `crate-duplicate` (`%refund%` is how many). At the default of `1` a
duplicate is a free re-roll rather than a loss.

An emote the player only owns through a permission is **not** a duplicate: it is unlocked for good, so
it outlives the rank that granted it.

## Settings

```yaml
crate:
  enabled: true
  start-keys: 1
  duplicate-refund: 1
  reward: UNLOCK
  blocks: []
  max-at-once: 4
  spin-frames: 34
  stagger-seconds: 2.0
```

| Key | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `enabled` | `true` | Off, the crate says so instead of opening. |
| `start-keys` | `1` | Keys a player is given the first time they are seen. |
| `duplicate-refund` | `1` | Keys handed back for something already owned. |
| `reward` | `UNLOCK` | `UNLOCK`, `ITEM` or `BOTH` — all unlock, for the reason above. |
| `blocks` | `[]` | The blocks that open the crate. Bind them in game, not by hand. |
| `max-at-once` | `4` | How many crates one player may open in one go. The screen fits seven. |
| `spin-frames` | `34` | How many faces the first reel runs through before it stops. Capped at 200, about eleven seconds. |
| `stagger-seconds` | `2.0` | How long after one reel stops the next one does. `0` stops them together. |

`on-spin`, `on-win` and `on-duplicate` are full ExyliaLib effect blocks — see
[Configuration](/docs/exyliaemotes/configuration#crate).

## Crate blocks

A physical block, anywhere in the world, that opens the crate when it is clicked:

```text
/emotesadmin crate block add      # the block you are looking at, up to six blocks away
/emotesadmin crate block remove
/emotesadmin crate block list
/emotesadmin crate block clear
```

They are written to `config.yml`, not into the world, so a bound block survives a schematic paste, a
world edit and a rollback. While it is bound it cannot be broken, blown up or pushed away, and clicking
it no longer does what its material does — a crate drawn on a barrel must not open a barrel.

## Handing emotes over without a crate

```text
/emotesadmin unlocks grant <player> <emote>
/emotesadmin unlocks revoke <player> <emote>
/emotesadmin unlocks clear <player>
```

The same unlock a crate writes, for a reward plugin, a support ticket or a wiped season. It outlives a
rank running out and needs no permissions plugin.

<Callout type="tip" title="Permissions and unlocks are two separate ways in">
A player may perform an emote they unlocked **or** one their rank grants. Selling a rarity as a rank is
one line — `exyliaemotes.tier.legendary` — and it is independent of anything they unboxed.
</Callout>
