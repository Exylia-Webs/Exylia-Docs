title: "Staff tools"
description: "Browsing anybody's vaults, read only or to edit, online, offline or on another server; emptying one, its history and putting a copy back, and the audit log."
---

Staff reach anybody's vaults with `/pvadmin` (`/playervaultsadmin`). The player may be online here,
online on another server or offline, as long as this server has seen them before; names are suggested as
you type.

| Command | Permission | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `/pvadmin list <player>` | `exyliaplayervaults.admin.view` | Their vault list. |
| `/pvadmin view <player> <number>` | `exyliaplayervaults.admin.view` | A vault as stored, read only. |
| `/pvadmin edit <player> <number>` | `exyliaplayervaults.admin.edit` | A vault, to change it. |
| `/pvadmin history <player> <number>` | `exyliaplayervaults.admin.history` | Its earlier copies. |
| `/pvadmin clear <player> <number>` | `exyliaplayervaults.admin.clear` | Empties it, after a confirmation. |

Every one of these is for players, not the console. `/pvadmin` on its own prints the usage, and
`/pvadmin reload` is on [Commands](/docs/exyliaplayervaults/commands#pvadmin).

## Somebody's list

`/pvadmin list <player>` opens the same screen the player sees, onto their vaults. Every number up to
`max-amount` — or up to the highest vault they have stored, when that is more — is drawn as open, because
staff are not limited by the owner's rank, and each one reads as a 54-slot vault.

What a click does depends on the staff member:

| Click | With `admin.edit` | With only `admin.view` |
|---|---|---|
| Left | Opens it to edit | Opens it read only |
| Right, shift-right | Renames it, changes its icon | Refused |

The header's bought count reads from memory, so it shows the right number only while the owner is online
on this server.

## Read only

`/pvadmin view <player> <number>` reads the vault straight from the database and shows it in a six-row
window titled `view-title`. Nothing can leave it: every click and drag is cancelled while it is on top,
the staff member's own inventory included, so not even a shift-click or a double-click collecting stacks
moves an item. It is a copy and is never written anywhere.

Because it takes no lock, it never disturbs whoever has the vault open. The flip side: a vault open right
now shows as of its last write, at most a checkpoint (30 seconds) behind.

## Editing

`/pvadmin edit <player> <number>` opens the vault in the same single window the owner would get, under
the same lock, always at six rows so stacks past the owner's rows are in sight. The number is not checked
against the owner's allowance, so staff can open, and fill, a vault the owner cannot reach yet.

When the vault is open somewhere:

- **on this server**, that window is written and closed, its viewer told *"Vault `<number>` was saved and
  closed because a member of staff is looking at it."*, and the vault opens for staff half a second later;
- **on another server**, that server is asked over Redis to write and close it. Staff are told *"Vault
  `<number>` was open on another server; it is being saved and closed."* and it opens for them a few
  seconds later. Without Redis the request does not travel, and staff are told it is in use until its viewer
  closes it.

The staff window is not held to `blocked-materials`, and it is not closed by combat. Opening it is
recorded in the audit log as `vault-open`, and when it closes, what changed is recorded as `vault-edit`
with the stacks added and removed.

## Emptying a vault

`/pvadmin clear <player> <number>` asks *"Empty vault `<number>` of `<player>`?"* and, on yes, empties
it. What it held is kept in its history first, so a clear can be undone. It is recorded as `vault-clear`.

A clear never runs while the vault is open: the window holding it is asked to close (here, or over Redis
elsewhere) and staff are told it is being saved and closed. Run the command again a moment later.

## History

Each vault keeps up to `history.keep` (10) earlier copies, newest first. A copy is what the vault held
**before** something changed it, filed under why:

| Cause | When |
|---|---|
| `CLOSED` | The owner closed the vault after changing it. |
| `EDITED` | Staff closed an edit window after changing it. |
| `CLEARED` | Staff emptied it. |
| `RESTORED` | Staff put an older copy back. |

A window closed without a change takes no copy, and an empty vault takes none on a clear or restore. Past
`keep`, the oldest copies are deleted; `0` keeps none.

`/pvadmin history <player> <number>` opens `menus/admin/history.yml`: one entry per copy with when it was
taken, how many stacks it held, the cause and who caused it.

- **Left-click** looks inside, in a read-only window titled `snapshot-title`.
- **Shift-right-click** puts that copy back, after the confirmation *"Put vault `<number>` of `<player>`
  back as it was `<when>`?"* It needs `exyliaplayervaults.admin.edit` as well as `admin.history`, because
  putting a copy back can bring back what the owner has taken out since.

A restore runs like a clear: never while the vault is open, and what the vault held is itself kept as a
`RESTORED` copy, so a restore can be undone too. It is recorded as `vault-restore`.

## The audit log

Staff actions and purchases go to ExyliaLib's audit log, read with `/exylialib logs`:

| Action | Recorded when |
|---|---|
| `vault-open` | Staff open somebody else's vault to edit. |
| `vault-edit` | That window closes changed, with what was added and removed. |
| `vault-view` | Staff look at a vault read only. |
| `vault-clear` | A vault is emptied. |
| `vault-restore` | A copy is put back, with its date. |
| `vault-buy` | A player buys a vault, with the price. |
| `vault-conflict` | A window had to be settled after another server wrote its vault. See [Network](/docs/exyliaplayervaults/network#when-two-servers-wrote-the-same-vault). |

Opening a list and looking inside a history copy are not recorded.
