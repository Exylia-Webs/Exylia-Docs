Supply drops are crates that fall out of the sky on a parachute and land as a real container anybody can run for.
Each one is announced first, lands where a beam marked the spot, stays locked for a moment and then opens to whoever
gets there. It vanishes with whatever is left once somebody empties it or its time runs out.

Everything is set up in game with `/supplydropsadmin` (alias `/envoysadmin`), or the **Supply drops** card of
`/warzoneadmin`. Two kinds of thing live there:

- **Tiers**: the kinds of crate. A name, a weight, a loot table, a look and, if you want, their own timings.
- **Envoys**: the areas crates fall into. An area, how many crates fall per run, which tiers it rolls and a timetable.

<Callout type="note" title="Nothing drops without an envoy">
The timetable, `/supplydrop start` and **Drop one now** all pick their spots inside an envoy's area. Until one envoy
has an area, only `/supplydrop here` drops anything.
</Callout>

## A drop, step by step

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Announced** | A chat announcement goes out, a beam of light rises over the spot and a boss bar counts down. 60 seconds by default. |
| **Falling** | The crate comes down under its tier's parachute, timed to touch down on the last second. |
| **Locked** | The crate is now a real block with a hologram over it. Opening it is refused with the time left. 20 seconds by default. |
| **Open** | Anybody can loot it. The first player to open it is announced. |
| **Gone** | Emptied, it goes as soon as the inventory is closed. Otherwise it vanishes with what is left when its despawn time, counted from the landing, runs out. 300 seconds by default. |

## Tiers

A network with no tiers yet gets three to start from, so the first envoy has something to drop:

| Tier | Weight | Chance | Canopy | Loot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `common` | 70 | 70% | White wool | Iron, gold, bread, arrows, ender pearls, experience bottles, sometimes diamonds. |
| `rare` | 25 | 25% | Magenta wool | Diamonds, emeralds, golden apples, experience bottles, netherite scrap, totems. |
| `legendary` | 5 | 5% | Yellow wool | Diamonds, netherite, enchanted golden apples, totems, a beacon, an elytra. |

**Tiers** lists every tier, heaviest first, with its weight, its chance against the other enabled tiers and how many
loot lines it has. The emerald creates one (type an id such as `legendary`); left-click a tier to open it, right-click
to delete it.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Turns the tier on or off. A disabled tier is never rolled. |
| **Name** | What players read in announcements and over the crate. Palette tokens such as `{highlight}` work. |
| **Weight** | How likely it is against the other enabled tiers: 70, 25 and 5 give 70%, 25% and 5%. A weight of 0 never comes up. |
| **Loot table** | The items a crate holds, in ExyliaLib's loot editor. |
| **Canopy** | The block the parachute is drawn with. |
| **Glow** | The outline colour of the beam, the falling crate and the unlock burst. |
| **Crate block** | What the crate lands as. Left-click picks a block, right-click follows `crate-block` in the config (a barrel). It must hold items: a barrel, a chest, a shulker box. |
| **Timings** | Its own announce, unlock and despawn times. See [Timings](#timings). |
| **Preview the fall** | Plays this tier's fall where you stand, for you alone. |
| **Delete** | Removes the tier and its loot table, after a confirmation. |

Each line of the loot table is rolled on its own, as a percentage chance, with an amount between its minimum and
maximum. When nothing comes up, one line is given anyway, so a crate never opens empty unless its table is. The items
land in random slots. Loot is items only: the editor offers no command or money lines.

## Envoys

An envoy is an area of the map where several crates fall together. It belongs to the server it was created on. A run
picks a spot for each crate inside the area, rolls a tier for each from the ones the envoy allows, announces them in
one message and lands them all at the same moment.

<Steps>
<Step title="Create it">
`/supplydropsadmin` → **Envoys** → the emerald. Type an id such as `desert-camp` and the envoy's screen opens.
</Step>
<Step title="Select the area">
Left-click **Area**. You are handed a golden axe: left-click a block for the first corner, right-click another for the
second, then shift + left-click to confirm.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose the crates and tiers">
**Crates** is how many fall each run (4 by default, up to 64). **Spacing** is the fewest blocks between two crates of
the same run (24). **Tiers**: left-click adds or removes one tier, right-click allows every tier again, which is the
default.
</Step>
<Step title="Give it a timetable">
**Timetable** opens ExyliaLib's schedule editor: clock times, days, a repeat every so often inside a window, and how
many players must be online. Once saved, the envoy runs by itself.
</Step>
<Step title="Try it">
**Run it now** drops it at once, as if its timetable had fired. Right-click **Area** to teleport to its middle.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="The corners set the height too">
Crates land on ground that lies between the heights of the two corners, searched from the upper one down. Click one
corner at or below the ground and the other well above it, or just under the roof for an area inside a building or a
cave. Two corners clicked on the same flat ground leave no room for a crate, and every run answers **No free spot
found**.
</Callout>

The other buttons: **Status** turns the envoy on or off (a disabled envoy never runs, by timetable or by hand), **Name**
is what players read when it starts, **Timings** holds its own times and **Delete** removes the envoy, its area and its
timetable and takes its crates away, after a confirmation. In the **Envoys** list, shift-click a row to run it now and
right-click to delete it.

### Where crates land

A spot is a random column of the area with solid ground (not bedrock) and two blocks of air on top, so never in water
or lava. It must be outside every ExyliaLib region, such as Warzone's own regen zones and duel rooms, outside every
ExyliaProtections claim, and at least **Spacing** away from the other crates of the run. Each crate tries up to
`attempts` (20) columns; a run that finds fewer spots than it wants drops the ones it found.

The spot is checked again on landing. If somebody built there in the meantime, the crate lands on top, up to 8 blocks
higher but never above the area; two crates of the same run on one column stack. A spot claimed since, or with no
room left, calls the crate off with an announcement. A block is never written over.

### The timetable

A timetable line that fires while the envoy still has crates out from its last run does nothing, and it is not
retried: the next run is the next time the timetable names. The schedule form also offers the `envoy-idle` gate,
which says the same thing. Times follow the `timezone` of ExyliaLib's `config.yml`.

## Timings

Every drop takes three times: how long it is announced, how long it stays locked, and how long it stays after landing.
Each is taken from the first place that sets it:

| Drop | Announce | Unlock and despawn |
|---|---|---|
| `/supplydrop start`, `/supplydrop here`, **Drop one now** | The time typed in the command, then the tier, the envoy, the config. | The tier, then the envoy, then the config. |
| An envoy run | The envoy, then the config, so every crate lands together. | The tier, then the envoy, then the config. |

On a **Timings** screen, left-click sets a time (typed as `30s`, `1m` or `2m30s`) and right-click clears it, so the
drop falls back to the next place in the table; a time taken from the config reads `(config)`. An envoy run is announced for at least 5 seconds, and a
crate always stays at least 10 seconds after landing. Keep the despawn time longer than the unlock time.

## What players see

| Moment | Announcement |
|---|---|
| One crate announced | Its tier, `x, z`, world and countdown, with a pointer to `/supplydrop`. |
| An envoy announced | The envoy's name, how many crates, the world and the countdown. |
| A crate lands | Its tier, `x, y, z` and when it unlocks. |
| First player opens it | Who reached it first. |
| Its time runs out | The crate vanished with what was left. |
| Its spot was taken | The crate was called off. |

Announcements reach every player who has not muted them, and the console. Each player mutes them in `/settings`, under
**Announcements**, as **Supply drops**.

While a drop is on its way, every player in its world sees one boss bar for the crate **closest to them**: its tier,
its `x, z` and the countdown. During an envoy, that is how each player knows which crate to run for. Muting does not
hide the bar; empty its text in the messages file to turn it off.

The beam, the fall, the landing, the unlock and the vanishing are display entities and particles, drawn for players
within `view-radius` (160 blocks) of the spot. A player who walks into range or logs back in during the countdown
still gets the beam. A crate dropping under a roof, anything within 12 blocks above the spot, skips the parachute and
simply lands. A hologram over a landed crate shows the tier and the countdown to unlock, then to vanishing.

`/supplydrop` (aliases `/drop`, `/supplydrops`) is open to every player: the tier and coordinates of the crate that is
out (with several, the one closest to its next moment), or when the next envoy falls.

## The crate

A landed crate cannot be broken, blown up, drained by a hopper or sold with a sell wand. When the module stops, every
crate is taken away with what is inside; one left standing by a crash is removed on the next start.

The first player to open a crate counts the `supply_drops.reached` statistic for ExyliaSurvivalCore's missions and
leaderboards, when it is installed. Who opened a crate and what each player took are written to the audit log
(`/exylialib logs`, actions `supply-drop-open` and `supply-drop-take`).

## Animation

**Animation** holds the five phases every drop is drawn with: **Marker** (the beam), **Fall**, **Impact**, **Unlock**
and **Vanish**. Each opens ExyliaLib's sequence editor and saves into the module's `config.yml`. In the lines,
`%glow%`, `%parachute%` and `%crate%` become the tier's colour, canopy block and crate block, and `%seconds%` how long
the marker lasts.

## Staff commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/supplydrop start [tier] [announce]` | One crate in a random enabled envoy's area. Without a tier it rolls from that envoy's tiers. It does not count as the envoy's run. |
| `/supplydrop here [tier] [announce]` | One crate on the block you stand on, without the region and claim check. In game only. |
| `/supplydrop envoy <envoy>` | Runs an envoy now. |
| `/supplydrop stop` | Takes every crate away at once, falling ones included, with what is inside. |

`announce` is a length such as `10s` or `2m`; to give one, name the tier first. A tier named in a command drops even
when it is disabled. On the admin screen, **Drop one now** is `start` and **Take every crate away** is `stop`, with a
confirmation. All of them need `exyliawarzone.supplydrops.admin`; see [Commands](/docs/exyliawarzone/commands) and
[Permissions](/docs/exyliawarzone/permissions).

## Limits and edge cases

- At most `max-active` (16) crates are out at once on a server, falling and landed, envoys included. A run is cut down
  to the room left.
- If every tier an envoy allows is disabled or deleted, its runs drop nothing. Right-click **Tiers** to allow them all.
- Tiers are shared by every server on the same database; envoys and their crates belong to one server. A tier edited
  on one server reaches the others when they restart.
- The look, the default timings and the limits are in `modules/supply-drops/config.yml`, also editable from the
  **Settings** button of `/warzoneadmin`. See [Configuration](/docs/exyliawarzone/configuration#supply-drops).
